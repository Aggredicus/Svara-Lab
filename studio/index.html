05 / Quick start

Learn by transforming

  1. Start with NAMASTE. Tap the seven tiles to hear individual notes.
  2. Choose Yaman and move Sa from C to D in the top bar. The phrase keeps its letters.
  3. Select Bhūpālī to hear a five-note rāga-safe route rather than inserting missing Ma or Ni.
  4. Use Compare keys to hear the same phrase at two tonic pitches.
  5. Enable With tāla to align the notes with a rhythmic cycle, or try the Sanskrit vowel keyboard.

Tāla atlas

Rhythm as architecture

Hindustani thekās, all 35 Sulādi Sapta tālas, plus cāpu cycles and subdivision experiments.

Hindustani: Mātrā = beat, vibhāg = group, sam = cycle landing, tālī = clap, khālī = wave/empty, thekā = recurring drum-bol pattern. Layakārī changes note density against the pulse.
Carnatic: Laghu (I) = 3/4/5/7/9 counts by jāti; drutam (O) = 2; anudrutam (U) = 1. Seven structures × five jāti = 35 tālas. Naḍai/gati is a separate subdivision choice.

Tradition & interpretation

Music, chakras & the image of sound

Select a svara to view one modern seven-chakra association and older animal metaphors.

The chakra–svara correspondence is symbolic and tradition-dependent, not a standard law of acoustic physiology. Some teachers reverse or alter the mappings. Colors, seed syllables and lotus-petal counts originate from wider tantric/yogic iconographies; they are not pitch frequencies, and listening to a note is not proven to activate a particular chakra.

Rāgamālā — painting the rāga

From the fifteenth century onward, Indian court ateliers developed rāgamālā painting cycles: visual “garlands of rāgas.” Personified singers, lovers, seasons, weather and deities carry the musical moods. This studio's original mandala and lotus artwork draws inspiration from those visual traditions; it is not a reproduction of a historical painting.

Explore The Met’s Ragamala: Picturing Sound and Ragas and Ragamalas.

Nāda, rasa, and śruti

Nāda refers to sound or resonance in Indian aesthetic and contemplative traditions. Rasa is aesthetic savor or emotional flavor in performance. Śruti in music concerns fine perceptible gradations of pitch; the classical 22-śruti discussion should not be mistaken for 22 equally spaced keyboard keys. This app defaults to a modern fixed-pitch piano approximation.

    Accuracy & technical disclosures

    Audio: The Web Audio API synthesizes pitched notes and percussion locally. No sample files, audio downloads or services are used. A4 defaults to 440 Hz; piano is 12-tone equal temperament. The optional just-ratio illustration is not a validated 22-śruti or rāga intonation system.

    Voice: Optional syllable speech uses the OS browser's speechSynthesis. Hindi/Sanskrit pronunciation and timing vary by installed voice; offline availability is not guaranteed. Turning it off leaves all notes and rhythms functional offline.

    Performance: Phrases are schematic and do not reproduce a teacher's meend, andolan or gamaka, nor full tabla timbres or konnakol articulation.

    Scale art: Mood, season and rāgamālā references are historically variable and tradition-specific. Original iconography for most entries is a modern visual teaching aid. The 12 Carnatic music-theory chakras are numeric groups, not the seven esoteric yogic chakras.

    Culture: Chakra mappings vary; symbolic correspondences are presented as interpretations, not clinical or physical facts. Hindustani and Carnatic terminology is kept distinct.

    Privacy: No accounts, trackers, analytics, external scripts, permissions, or requests. Only local practice best-score and preferences are saved in your browser (if permitted).

    License: Original application code and vector artwork © 2026 Svara Studio contributors, released under the MIT License. Referenced material remains with its respective authors; no recordings or copyrighted imagery are embedded.

    Research notes

    Nāṭyaśāstra chapter 28 describes seven svaras and older grāma/śruti systems; the modern standard 72-mēḷakartā naming and 35-Sulādi tables were formalized over a much longer historical period. Do not retrofit those modern tables into the early text.

    Hindustani rāga details and representative phrases vary across gharānās. The catalog uses short pedagogical interpretations and links to Darbar Foundation materials for further study.
