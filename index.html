OFFLINE GUIDE

01. Compose Sanskrit

Tap a large key for the center letter. Touch, then flick ← ↑ → ↓ to choose its alternate. Audio starts once after lifting; you can also tap the visible consonant atlas to enter any letter. Switch on Practice to hear without typing. By default, left thumb: consonants; right thumb: vowels. Check InScript-inspired hands to swap these sides to resemble the standard vowel-left / consonant-right arrangement. Each traditional five-consonant varga uses tap = first, left = aspirated, up = voiced, right = voiced aspirated, down = nasal. The other consonants use remaining directions. This is a touch adaptation, not an exact InScript mapping. Holding reveals a directional compass instead of cramming symbols onto the key.

To make रा, flick the YA family to र, then select आ. To join consonants, press JOIN (्) before the next consonant.

02. Type or paste text

The text area above the keyboard is editable. Use a physical keyboard, phone input method or paste Devanāgarī or IAST. Thumb-key strokes append to the text. The composition is saved locally in your browser.

Try: राम, कृष्ण, ॐ नमः, or rāma. Use Undo Key to undo a gesture and backspace to edit directly in the text area.

03. Play the full phrase

Press ▶ Play text to hear the whole composition in order. Adjust PACE to change syllable timing, ■ Stop text to stop, or ↓ Text WAV to export the rendered phrase as a mono WAV for your DAW.

This version models vowels with the existing formant engine and consonants with simplified noise, voicing and nasal cues. It is an experimental synthesizer, not an accurate Sanskrit pronunciation teacher.

04. Hold and express

In Perform, touch and hold the large voice pad with one finger. Drag the XY field with another: horizontal = ±12 semitone pitch; vertical = intensity. Enable Lock Sustain to release your first finger. On phones, the bottom rail keeps pitch and intensity accessible across tabs.

The big voice button keeps its dimensions and text steady while active. Press All voices off to silence everything.

05. Sculpt, export and MIDI

Sculpt adjusts pitch, brightness, breath, vibrato, tract shape, glottal opening, attack and release. Export renders 14 vowel WAVs with loops, note metadata and manifest. Patch library stores your synthesis settings as JSON. MIDI input works in compatible browsers.

06. Boundaries & privacy

One HTML file, runs offline after you save it. Audio is generated on your device; the text isn't sent to a server. Best-supported inputs are Devanāgarī and conventional IAST. Vedic accent marks, sandhi, consonant timing, and rare vocalic liquids remain approximations. Exported text WAV is monophonic and not a phonetic reference recording.

Suggested test: type रामः, play, vary pitch and volume, export WAV, then listen in your DAW.

THE SOURCE / FILTER MODEL

A phase-driven, band-limited glottal-like source passes through three time-varying band-pass resonators. Air noise, spectral tilt, vibrato, and slowly morphing diphthongs add character. It is deliberately reminiscent of early singing computers—not a recording or a neural speech model.

Phonetic caution: ṛ, ṝ, ḷ and ḹ are syllabic liquids; their exact traditional realizations vary. Three resonances alone cannot fully reproduce liquid articulation or nasal/retroflex transitions. Treat these as auditionable timbre sketches until validated against real Sanskrit-speaking voices.

DAW tips: Load a _LOOP.wav onto your sampler, set its root note according to manifest.json, enable sustain looping if necessary, and set your sampler's release envelope to taste. Some DAWs ignore RIFF smpl markers; loop regions are also in the manifest. Use _SHOT.wav for non-looping pads.

PERFORM WITH YOUR FINGERTIPS

Press and hold a pad to sustain it. Flip on Latch pads for hands-free knob sculpting. A connected MIDI keyboard controls pitch while using the selected vowel. Move a knob during a held note to crossfade into the updated synthesis. Standard browser audio requires an initial tap before playback.

All synthesis, ZIP creation, WAV encoding, and audio checks run in this single HTML file using standard browser APIs. Your presets are exportable JSON. Validation checks technical integrity—not human-rated pronunciation. Reference concepts: Klatt synthesis, Sanskrit vowel matrix.
